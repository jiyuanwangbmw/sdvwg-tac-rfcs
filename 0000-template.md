# Motivation
[motivation]: #motivation

Any change within the Eclipse SDV WG TAC's responsibilities should focus on a
concrete problem faced by the Working Group, its projects, PMCs, releases, or
other stakeholders. This section should explain this problem in detail,
including necessary background.

It should also describe concrete scenarios that show how the proposal affects
stakeholders and addresses the problem. These scenarios can then guide the
development and evaluation of the proposal.

This section is one of the most important sections of any RFC and can be
lengthy.

# Stakeholder impact
[stakeholder-impact]: #stakeholder-impact

Explain the proposal as if it had already been adopted and you were teaching it
to a TAC member, Eclipse project contributor, PMC member, or other affected
stakeholder. That generally means:

- Introducing new named concepts.
- Explaining the change largely in terms of examples.
- Explaining how affected stakeholders should *think* about the change and how
  it affects their work. Describe the impact as concretely as possible.
- If applicable, provide transition, rollout, or adoption guidance.
- If applicable, describe the differences between adopting this in existing
  projects or processes and starting with it in new ones.
- Discuss how this affects the ability to understand, apply, and maintain the
  relevant roadmap, policy, process, release plan, or technical artifact.

For action-oriented RFCs, focus on how contributors should carry out the
proposal and give examples of its concrete impact. For policy RFCs, provide an
example-driven introduction to the policy and explain its effect on projects,
PMCs, releases, and other stakeholders.

# Detailed proposal
[detailed-proposal]: #detailed-proposal

Explain the proposal in sufficient detail that:

- Its interaction with existing policies, processes, plans, and technical work
  is clear.
- Any follow-on actions, dependencies, and responsibilities are clear.
- Edge cases and exceptions are examined through examples.
- Any interaction with the Eclipse SDV Working Group Charter or other applicable
  Eclipse Foundation processes is explicit.
- Any additional approval or action required from a person, committee, or other
  governing body is identified.

The section should return to the examples given in the previous section and
explain more fully how the detailed proposal addresses them.

# Rationale and alternatives
[rationale-and-alternatives]: #rationale-and-alternatives

- Why is this proposal the best among the available options?
- What alternatives have been considered, and why were they not chosen?
- What is the impact of not doing this?
- Does the proposed change make the affected artifacts or processes easier or
  harder to understand, apply, and maintain?
- Does the proposal strengthen or weaken the Working Group's ability to achieve
  its chartered objectives?

# Prior art
[prior-art]: #prior-art

Discuss prior art, both the good and the bad, in relation to this proposal.
A few examples of what this can include are:

- For artifact proposals: Have comparable automotive or open source ecosystems
  used a similar artifact, and what did they learn?
- For governance or process proposals: How have other communities addressed the
  same need?
- For coordination proposals: What can be learned from comparable multi-project
  release and integration efforts?
- Papers: Are there any published papers or great posts that discuss this?
  If you have some relevant papers to refer to, this can serve as a more detailed
  theoretical background.

This section is intended to encourage you as an author to consider lessons from
other communities and provide readers of your RFC with a fuller picture.

If there is no prior art, that is fine - your ideas are interesting to us whether
they are brand new or adapted from other communities.

Precedent can support a proposal, but it does not on its own motivate an RFC.
Explain why the precedent fits the Eclipse SDV WG TAC's scope and constraints.

# Unresolved questions
[unresolved-questions]: #unresolved-questions

- What parts of the proposal must be resolved before the TAC considers its
  disposition?
- What details may be resolved through follow-on actions?
- What related issues are out of scope for this RFC but could be addressed
  independently in the future?

# Future possibilities
[future-possibilities]: #future-possibilities

Think about what the natural extension and evolution of your proposal would
be and how it would affect the Working Group, projects, PMCs, coordinated
releases, and other stakeholders. Use this section to consider interactions
with the TAC's other responsibilities and the Working Group's technical roadmap.

This is also a good place to record related ideas that are out of scope for the
current RFC.

If you have tried and cannot think of any future possibilities,
you may simply state that you cannot think of anything.

Note that having something written down in the future-possibilities section
is not a reason to accept the current or a future RFC; such notes should be
in the section on motivation or rationale in this or subsequent RFCs.
The section merely provides additional information.
